Windows: Duolingo 30 Sep–26 Oct (27 posts), Babbel 31 Aug–21 Oct (28), Busuu 23 Mar–21 Oct (26). Posts younger than 72 hours and pinned old posts left out.

## What each audience asks for

- **Duolingo:** what the referenced show is, credit for the original creators, and help with lost streaks ([comment report](comment-mining-duolingo-outlier.md)).
- **Babbel** (60 comments on its best post, 2,009 total): people share which Indigenous language their family speaks and ask where to learn it. "yo quiero aprender zapoteco, maya y nautl donde puedo aprender?" and "Pueden hacer uno de purhepecha". Several mention grandparents who never taught them: "My grandmother spoke Nahuatl but she never wanted to teach it to us or even speak it in front of us."
- **Busuu:** median 2 comments per post; too few to read a signal. Skipped to save credits.

## Patterns that work across the group

1. **Comparison beats instruction.** Babbel's top posts (2.09M, 711k, 584k, 543k views) all compare how the same thing is said across countries or languages. Its English "Romance languages" explainers sit near its median.
2. **Carousels about Spanish-speaking regions outperform for Babbel.** Three of its four biggest posts are photo carousels, and two are captioned in Spanish for a Mexican audience.
3. **Personality over lessons for Duolingo.** Its winners teach nothing; they are mascot skits tied to a reference.

## Gaps

1. **Indigenous and heritage languages.** Babbel's best post in the window is about them, and its commenters ask where to learn them. None of the three accounts teaches them in the sampled posts. (Babbel's bio lists Spanish, English, French, Italian "and 10 other languages".)
2. **Busuu is close to inactive** on TikTok (under one post a week, 0.4% reach). Its share of this audience is open.

## Ideas

1. A series comparing one phrase across Spanish, Nahuatl, Zapotec and Maya, with a native speaker for each.
2. "My grandma speaks ___ and I don't": heritage-learner stories, which the comments are full of.
3. Region-vs-region carousels ("what is this called in your country?"), the format behind three of Babbel's top four posts.
